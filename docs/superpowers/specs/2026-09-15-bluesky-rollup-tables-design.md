# Bluesky rollups: tables the database keeps current, and a grain for every chart range

The API's coarse grains read views that re-add every minute in the range on
each request. This replaces them with rollup tables that Postgres triggers
keep current as the pipeline writes each minute, serves six grains sized so
every chart gets a sensible number of points, and bounds every response
before it runs. The migration and the grain SQL are generated by
`sqlflow rollup` from one declaration, `rollups.yml`.

Depends on sql-flow's rollup toolchain, designed in
`sql-flow/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-15-rollups-design.md`, and on the two
serve changes it depends on: turbolytics/sql-flow#282 (range and grain
selection) and integer param bounds. Implementation here waits on the
sql-flow release that ships all three.

## Why

The API went up on Render on 2026-09-15 and was load tested against the
production database, 2.5 days of minutes:

| Request | Median from a client, 20 requests one at a time |
|---|---|
| `pipeline_status` | 214 ms |
| `posts_by_lang`, `5m`, default 12 h | 140 ms |
| `posts_by_lang`, `1h`, default 7 d | 1.5 s |
| `posts_by_lang`, `1d`, default 30 d | 1.5 s |

Throughput stayed at 1.1 to 1.2 requests per second at 4, 8 and 16
concurrent clients, because serve runs one query at a time. At 16 clients,
66% of requests timed out after 10 s, `5m` included. The worker kept writing
during the test: every minute from 10:26 to 10:40 landed, the last write at
10:42:07.

Two costs grow with history. Each view re-adds every minute in its range, so
`1h` and `1d` read the most rows while returning the fewest. And
`pipeline_status` scans the whole minute table.

The grains also did not fit a chart. Seven days at `5m` is 2,016 points per
series, more than a chart has pixels, and past `max_rows` at the top 10
languages. An hour at `5m` is 12 points.

## Decisions

| Decision | Choice | Rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Where the aggregation runs | Rollup tables in Postgres, kept current by generated triggers on the pipeline's minute writes. | Views: cost follows the range. A window per grain in the pipeline: a restart overwrites the open hour and day. A cache in serve: memory on a small box, and logic in serve. |
| Grains | `1m`, `5m`, `15m`, `1h`, `6h`, `1d`. | `5m`, `1h`, `1d`: 12 points for an hour, and a range just past 1 day drops to 24 points. `1m`, `5m`, `1h`, `1d`: drops to 24 and 30 points at the grain changes. |
| Points per response | At most 365 buckets. Each grain's widest range is its width × about 300. | Unbounded: 7 d at `5m` answered 22,176 rows. |
| `top` | Default 10, at most 20, refused outside 1 to 20. | At most 50, clamped: 365 × 51 rows exceeds `max_rows`, and a clamp answers less than was asked. |
| Table names | New: `posts_by_lang_5m` … `posts_by_lang_1d`, `posts_total_5m` … `posts_total_1d`. The views are dropped in a later migration, after the deploy is verified. | Reusing the view names: dropping a view and creating a table in its place leaves a window where an API instance mid-deploy reads a relation that is gone. |
| `pipeline_status` | The same view and columns, read from the `posts_total` rollup and the minute table's key. | A full scan of the minute table per request. |

## Grains

Six grains, each serving up to its widest range. With no `grain`, #282 picks
the finest one whose range covers the request.

| Grain | Reads | Widest range | Points at widest | Points just past the grain before |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1m` | `posts_per_minute_by_lang` | 6 h | 360 | |
| `5m` | `posts_by_lang_5m` | 1 d | 288 | 72 |
| `15m` | `posts_by_lang_15m` | 3 d | 288 | 96 |
| `1h` | `posts_by_lang_1h` | 14 d | 336 | 72 |
| `6h` | `posts_by_lang_6h` | 90 d | 360 | 56 |
| `1d` | `posts_by_lang_1d` | 365 d | 365 | 90 |

What a page's usual ranges get:

| Range | Grain | Points |
|---|---|---|
| last hour | `1m` | 60 |
| last 6 hours | `1m` | 360 |
| last 24 hours, the default | `5m` | 288 |
| last 7 days | `1h` | 168 |
| last 30 days | `6h` | 120 |

A response holds at most 365 buckets × 21 series (the top 20 and `other`),
7,665 rows, under `max_rows` of 10,000. `sqlflow rollup check` proves it, so
`truncated` is never true.

## `rollups.yml`

New, at the repository root. Its content is the example in sql-flow's spec:
the source `posts_per_minute_by_lang`, the six-grain ladder, two dimension
sets, and the `posts_by_lang` served dataset.

- `posts_by_lang`: dimension `lang`, measure `posts` as a sum. Serves the
  chart.
- `posts_total`: no dimension, measures `posts` as a sum and `minutes` as
  `count_buckets`. Serves `pipeline_status`.

## Migrations

### `0005_rollups.sql`

Generated: `sqlflow rollup ddl -c rollups.yml > migrations/0005_rollups.sql`.
Not edited by hand; CI compares it with the generator's output.

It locks the minute table, creates the ten rollup tables, their trigger
functions and triggers, and backfills both `5m` tables from the minutes,
which cascades to every coarser grain. `bin/migrate.sh` runs it in one
transaction. The worker's next write waits for the backfill, under a second at
today's 3,500 minutes.

### `0006_pipeline_status_from_rollups.sql`

Hand-written. The same columns and types, so `CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW` accepts
it and the demo page's contract does not change:

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW pipeline_status AS
SELECT
  (SELECT min(bucket) FROM posts_per_minute_by_lang) AS first_bucket,
  (SELECT max(bucket) FROM posts_per_minute_by_lang) AS latest_bucket,
  (SELECT max(updated_at) FROM posts_per_minute_by_lang
     WHERE bucket >= (SELECT max(bucket) FROM posts_per_minute_by_lang) - INTERVAL '1 hour')
                                                     AS last_write_at,
  (SELECT coalesce(sum(minutes), 0)::bigint FROM posts_total_1d) AS minutes_observed,
  (SELECT coalesce(sum(posts), 0)::bigint FROM posts_total_1d)   AS total_posts;
```

`min` and `max` on `bucket` read one end of the primary key. `last_write_at`
reads only the last hour of minutes. `updated_at` is a minute's first write,
so the newest one is always among recent buckets. The two sums read one row
per day. The `::bigint` casts keep each column's type, since `sum` over
`bigint` returns `numeric`.

### Later: `0007_drop_rollup_views.sql`

A follow-up PR, after the deploy is verified: drops `posts_per_5m_by_lang`,
`posts_per_hour_by_lang`, `posts_per_day_by_lang` and the three expression
indexes from `0004`. Those indexes cost every minute write and nothing reads
them once the API reads the tables.

## `serve.yml`

`posts_by_lang` becomes the dataset `sqlflow rollup serve -c rollups.yml`
prints: params `since`, `until`, `lang`, and `top` with `min: 1, max: 20`;
`range: {since: since, until: until, default: 24h}`; and the six grains with
their `max_range`. The SQL reads the rollup tables with `$since` and `$until`
bound directly.

`pipeline_status` stays hand-written and unchanged: it reads the view.

`commands`, `http`, `auth` and `limits` do not change.

## Makefile

- `rollups`: regenerates `migrations/0005_rollups.sql` and prints the serve
  dataset to paste into `serve.yml`.
- `validate` also runs `rollup check` against the pinned image.

## CI

`image` job:

- `sqlflow rollup check -c rollups.yml --migration migrations/0005_rollups.sql --serve serve.yml`
  against the pinned image.

`migrations` job:

- The schema check selects from the ten rollup tables with `WHERE false`, in
  place of the three views.
- The UTC check inserts minutes either side of UTC midnight and reads all five
  rollup grains of `posts_by_lang` from an `Asia/Kolkata` session. Every
  bucket sits on a UTC boundary.
- New: after inserting minutes in two languages across two days, then
  republishing one minute with a new count, every rollup table equals a
  `GROUP BY` of the minute table computed in the check, and `pipeline_status`
  equals the same figures computed from the minute table.
- The index-use check for the views is removed with the views in `0007`.

`api` job:

- The listing has `posts_by_lang` with grains `15m`, `1d`, `1h`, `1m`, `5m`,
  `6h`, and each `max_range`.
- `grain=1h&top=1` over one hour still returns `en` as the sum and `ja` as
  `other`, and `lang=ja` returns `ja` unfolded, now from the rollup tables.
- Without `grain`, a 1-hour range answers with `grain: "1m"`, a 2-day range
  with `15m`, and a 30-day range with `6h`. Without a range, the answer covers
  24 hours at `5m`. The old `missing_grain` assertion goes: a ranged dataset
  needs no grain.
- `grain=5m` with a 7-day range is `400 range_too_wide`.
- `top=0` and `top=21` are `400 invalid_param`.
- `pipeline_status` counts the inserted minutes and posts, as today.

## README

"Query it over HTTP" is the page's contract and changes with it:

- The grains table: six grains, each widest range, and the selection rule.
- `since` and `until` default to the last 24 hours. `grain` is optional.
- `top`: default 10, 1 to 20, else `400 invalid_param`.
- The status table gains `range_too_wide` under `400`.
- `truncated` never happens, and why.
- New curl output from a real run.

"What gets stored" gains the rollup tables and the triggers that keep them,
and loses the views after `0007`.

## Verification

Before the PR:

- `make validate` passes, including `rollup check`.
- CI passes every job above.

Before the deploy:

- `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` against the production database for today's
  `1h` and `1d` view queries, recorded in the PR. It confirms the view's
  re-aggregation is where the 1.5 s goes.
- A baseline: 20 sequential requests per range below, recording each
  response's `elapsed_ms`.

After the deploy:

- The worker's log shows no `table manager failed`, and `pipeline_status`'s
  `last_write_at` keeps advancing.
- 20 sequential requests for each usual range above and each grain's widest
  range: server-side `elapsed_ms` p95 at or under 250 ms for every one. A
  grain that misses gets a narrower `max_range`, not a cache.
- Every rollup total equals the minute table's for the same range, checked
  with psql against the production database.

Concurrency, and the load test that failed on 2026-09-15, belong to the
connection pool spec that follows.

## Out of scope

- The session pool and serve metrics. Next spec.
- Caching. After the pool, and only if the budget is missed.
- Dropping the views: `0007`, a follow-up PR.
- Retention of minutes. The rollups outlive it by design, since deletes do
  not propagate.
- The demo page.
